# biopharma-innovation in the US

Firm-level returns on research and design spending and the number of patents for any given firm in the biopharmaceutical industry is not an adequate way gauge innovation for the biopharmaceutical sector. This is mainly because the risks are not even among different companies.
* large and established firms may be less subject to risk than new firms, depending on the context.
* the biopharmaceutical industry has become one of the most competitive industries mainly because massive technology advances over the past 40 years.
* although the number of patents per firm has been decreasing for some time, the annual number of patents submitted for future FDA regulation is 3x higher in 2025 than the number of patens submitted in 2019 and 100X higher than the number of patents submitted in 2010.


### drivers of innovation 

* the number of ingredients used has increased substantially since the FDA started releasing information to the public in 1980s.
* the number of ways a drug is consumed has also increased in a similar manner.
* the barriers to entry are not as high as in the previous century. technology has made it easier for new firms to enter and make large contribution fast, compared to new biopharmaceutical companies in the late 80s and early 90s. the barriers of entry into the industry are still very high compared to other industries.
* the pool of investors for the industry has grown tremendously since the 1980s.
* updates in regulation have also made the industry more competitive. the Orphan Drug Act of 1983 and the Hatch–Waxman Act of 1984 seemingly made the industry more competitive overnight by allowing producers of bring their products to market quicker.
